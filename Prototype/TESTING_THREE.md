# Prototype #3
At last, the self-balancing robot has come to life. Prototype 4 is the first to show functionality across the three basic tests I've outlined with the robot's problem statement. This is the first version of the robot to do so, and due to its testing success, this is the final self-balancing robot prototype.

The first test was the balancing capability test, that demands 60 seconds of continuously-sustained balancing from the prototype. The first and third trials ran for around 100 seconds, both of which were better than all testing data from the three previous prototypes. The second, fourth, and fifth trials did not fall at all; they found a "balance point" where the motors simply did not move due to the prototype finding an angle of perfect balance. This is a dramatic jump from previous prototypes, as they continued oscillating until eventually falling down.

The disturbance recovery tests are definitely the hardest of the three functionality tests, and prototype 4 was the first to show a real cascade-PID response rather than just static balance. Across five "tap" trials, it recovered after wobbling for three tests while falling for two. For the three successful trials, there was a consistent five-second stabilizing window to re-stabilize the robot. This is a meaningful shift from the previous prototypes, despite not consistently succeeding these tests, as the previous bots had no corrective response at all or overcorrected into a fall in the opposite direction. It's a success with limitations; the caliber of motors and chassis that this project's limited budget allows for cannot be pushed further.

Terrain performance was the clearest improvement in regards to testing the four prototypes. Prototype 4 was able to pass on an even surface like hardwood, a relatively-even wool carpet, and a bumpy table carpet. Prototypes 1 and 2 failed everywhere and prototype 3 only succeeded on the hardwood, as shown in the video. Prototype 4, however succeeded on the bumpy terrain as well, as seen in its own video. The previous prototype's flat-body design was simply too short to allow for a little oscillation in those bumpy terrains; bringing the center of mass a bit higher was the meaningful change here.

Taken together, prototype 4 has crossed from "technically balancing" into "can balance under real-world variation." It has passed tests that involved sustained balancing, disturbances, and rough terrains, which is a great sign that the underlying tuning, design, and construction is solid. This marks the end of my self-balancing robot project.
